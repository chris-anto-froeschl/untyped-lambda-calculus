\begin{abstract}
Alpha equivalence of lambda terms with named variables is defined in
the literature in several ways: by an axiom for renaming a bound
variable, under one of two side conditions on the new variable, or by
rules that swap the two bound variables with a third one. Crole proved
in detail that five such definitions give the same relation, and
observed that published proof sketches of this folklore fact are
sometimes incorrect. We present a formalization of Crole's theorem in
the Lean~4 proof assistant, developed for the library CSLib. To our
knowledge the theorem had not been mechanized before. The
formalization supplies the proofs that Crole omits or only sketches.
As a second result we formalize, following Norrish and Vestergaard,
an isomorphism between named terms modulo alpha equivalence and pure
de Bruijn terms, with their respective notions of beta-reduction. It
connects the two representations of lambda terms within one library:
through it, the Church--Rosser theorem for de Bruijn terms becomes a
theorem about named terms. The statements in this paper are linked to
their Lean sources.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A machine-checked formalization of Crole's theorem that five
  definitions of alpha equivalence coincide.
\item An isomorphism, formalized in Lean~4, between a de Bruijn
  representation and the quotient of named terms by alpha
  equivalence, following Norrish and Vestergaard's proof pearl.
\item A roadmap toward a formalized foundation for untyped lambda
  calculus research, including further binding representations and
  the remaining formalizations in Norrish and Vestergaard's network
  of directly proved relationships.
\end{highlights}

\section*{Data availability}
The Lean~4 formalization discussed in this paper is developed for
CSLib (\url{https://github.com/leanprover/cslib}). The parts that are
not yet merged there are under review in the pull requests cited in
the text, and available at \url{\LeanRepo}, at the revisions that the
links in this paper point to. A blueprint of both
formalizations, with a dependency graph showing their connections, is
at \url{https://chris-anto-froeschl.github.io/untyped-lambda-calculus/}.
