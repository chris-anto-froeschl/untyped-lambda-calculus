\begin{abstract}
Alpha equivalence of $\lambda$-terms with named variables is defined
either by renaming bound variables or by swapping the bound variables
of two abstractions with the same fresh variable, and each style comes
in several variants. That these definitions agree is widely
accepted, but Crole observed that published proof sketches of this
fact are sometimes incorrect. He provided a pen-and-paper proof that several such
definitions give the same relation. We formalize Crole's theorem in
the Lean~4 proof assistant, as part of the library CSLib. To our
knowledge the theorem had not been mechanized before. The
formalization supplies the proofs that Crole omits or only sketches.
The paper references earlier sources for the now formalized lemmas in question. As a second
result we formalize, following Norrish and Vestergaard, an isomorphism
between named terms modulo $\alpha$-equivalence and pure de Bruijn terms,
with their respective notions of $\beta$-reduction. By the first result it
does not matter which of these definitions the quotient is taken by.
Through the isomorphism, the Church--Rosser theorem for de Bruijn
terms becomes a theorem about named terms, and the two separately developed representations
of $\lambda$-terms are connected within one library. Both results are
part of CSLib, where further work on the untyped $\lambda$-calculus can
build on them. The statements in this paper are linked to their
Lean sources.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Formalizes the proof that common definitions of $\alpha$-equivalence
  coincide.
\item Formalizes the isomorphism of de Bruijn terms and $\alpha$-quotiented
  named terms.
\item Connects the named and de Bruijn APIs of CSLib, so results transfer between them.
\end{highlights}

\section*{Data availability}
The Lean~4 formalization discussed in this paper is developed for
CSLib (\url{https://github.com/leanprover/cslib}). The parts that are
not yet merged there are under review in the pull requests cited in
the text, and available at \url{\LeanRepo}, at the revisions that the
links in this paper point to. A blueprint of both
formalizations, with a dependency graph showing their connections, is
at \url{https://chris-anto-froeschl.github.io/untyped-lambda-calculus/}.
