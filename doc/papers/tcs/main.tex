\begin{abstract}
Alpha equivalence of lambda terms with named variables has several
definitions in the literature: the congruence generated by renaming a
bound variable, under one of two side conditions on the new variable,
and syntax-directed rules that swap the bound variables of two
abstractions with a third one. That these definitions agree is widely
accepted, but Crole observed that published proof sketches of this
fact are sometimes incorrect, and proved in detail that five such
definitions give the same relation. We formalize Crole's theorem in
the Lean~4 proof assistant, as part of the library CSLib. To our
knowledge the theorem had not been mechanized before. The
formalization supplies the proofs that Crole omits or only sketches,
and we list earlier sources for the lemmas it needs. As a second
result we formalize, following Norrish and Vestergaard, an isomorphism
between named terms modulo alpha equivalence and pure de Bruijn terms,
with their respective notions of beta-reduction; by Crole's theorem it
does not matter which of the five definitions the quotient is taken
by. Through the isomorphism, the Church--Rosser theorem for de Bruijn
terms becomes a theorem about named terms, and the two representations
of lambda terms are connected within one library. Both results are
first pieces of a formalized foundation for research on the untyped
lambda calculus. The statements in this paper are linked to their
Lean sources.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Machine-checked proof that Crole's five definitions of alpha
  equivalence coincide.
\item Formalized isomorphism of de Bruijn terms and named terms modulo
  alpha equivalence.
\item A roadmap toward a formalized foundation for untyped lambda
  calculus research.
\end{highlights}

\section*{Data availability}
The Lean~4 formalization discussed in this paper is developed for
CSLib (\url{https://github.com/leanprover/cslib}). The parts that are
not yet merged there are under review in the pull requests cited in
the text, and available at \url{\LeanRepo}, at the revisions that the
links in this paper point to. A blueprint of both
formalizations, with a dependency graph showing their connections, is
at \url{https://chris-anto-froeschl.github.io/untyped-lambda-calculus/}.
