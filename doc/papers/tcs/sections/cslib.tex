\section{Named lambda terms in CSLib}
\label{sec:cslib}

CSLib currently represents untyped lambda terms in a standard named
style: variables are drawn from an infinite set of atoms,
and terms are built from variables, application, and abstraction.
Our notation follows Crole~\cite{crole2012a}, except that we write
the concrete syntax of lambda terms where Crole writes
$P(E_1, E_2)$ and $B([a]E)$ for a generic binary constructor and a
generic binder; Crole notes that his proofs are insensitive to this
choice~\cite[Section~2]{crole2012a}.

\begin{definition}[Named lambda terms]
\label{def:named-terms}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Basic.lean\#L38}
Let $\Atoms$ be a set of atoms. The set $\Lam$ of named lambda terms
is generated by
\[
  M \;::=\; a \mid M\, M \mid \lam{a}{M}
  \qquad (a \in \Atoms).
\]
We write $\occ(M)$
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Basic.lean\#L59}
for the finite set of all atoms occurring in $M$, including binding
occurrences, and $\free(M)$
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Basic.lean\#L47}
for the finite set of atoms occurring free in $M$, and abbreviate
\[
  a \nocc M \;\iff\; a \notin \occ(M)
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  a \fresh M \;\iff\; a \notin \free(M).
\]
In the second case we say that $a$ is \emph{fresh} for $M$. Both
notations extend to lists, so that $z \nocc a, b, M, M'$ means that $z$
differs from $a$ and $b$ and occurs in neither $M$ nor $M'$.
\end{definition}

Crole assumes $\Atoms$ to be countable, with equality of atoms as its
only operation. In CSLib, $\Atoms$ is an arbitrary type with decidable
equality that comes with a function $\freshatom$ from finite sets of
atoms to atoms such that $\freshatom(S) \notin S$; in particular
$\Atoms$ is infinite, and an atom avoiding any given finite set can be
computed.

Three operations act on the atoms of a term. The first is purely
textual and is the one CSLib's baseline alpha equivalence is phrased
with.

\begin{definition}[Renaming]
\label{def:rename}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Basic.lean\#L66}
$\ren{M}{a}{b}$ is $M$ with every occurrence of $a$, free, bound or
binding, replaced by $b$.
\end{definition}

The second applies a bijection on atoms to every atom of a term,
binding occurrences included.

\begin{definition}[Permutation action {\cite[Section~2]{crole2012a}}]
\label{def:perm-action}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/AlphaEquivDefs.lean\#L68}
For a permutation $\pi$ of $\Atoms$, the term $\pi \act M$ is defined by
\[
  \pi \act a = \pi(a)
  \qquad
  \pi \act (M_1\, M_2) = (\pi \act M_1)\,(\pi \act M_2)
  \qquad
  \pi \act (\lam{a}{M}) = \lam{\pi(a)}{\pi \act M}.
\]
For the transposition $\sw{a}{b}$ we call $\sw{a}{b} \act M$ the
\emph{swap}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/AlphaEquivDefs.lean\#L80}
of $a$ and $b$ in $M$. The \emph{agreement set}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/AlphaEquivDefs.lean\#L60}
of two permutations is
$\AS(\pi, \pi') = \{\, a \in \Atoms \mid \pi(a) = \pi'(a) \,\}$.
\end{definition}

Unlike renaming (Definition~\ref{def:rename}), a swap is a bijection
on terms and never identifies two atoms. The two operations agree
when the new atom is unused: if $z \nocc M$ then
$\sw{z}{a} \act M = \ren{M}{a}{z}$
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Properties.lean\#L159}.

The third is capture-avoiding substitution, which Crole writes
$E\{a'/a\}$ for the substitution of an atom and which we write
$\subst{M}{a}{N}$ for the substitution of an arbitrary term.

\begin{definition}[Capture-avoiding substitution]
\label{def:subst}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Basic.lean\#L112}
\begin{align*}
  \subst{b}{a}{N} &=
    \begin{cases} N & \text{if } b = a\\ b & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\\
  \subst{(M_1\, M_2)}{a}{N} &= \subst{M_1}{a}{N}\; \subst{M_2}{a}{N}\\
  \subst{(\lam{b}{M})}{a}{N} &=
    \begin{cases}
      \lam{b}{M} & \text{if } b = a\\
      \lam{b}{\subst{M}{a}{N}} & \text{if } b \neq a \text{ and } b \fresh N\\
      \lam{c}{\subst{\ren{M}{b}{c}}{a}{N}} & \text{otherwise, where }
        c = \freshatom(\occ(M) \cup \occ(N) \cup \{a, b\})
    \end{cases}
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[The three operations]
\label{ex:operations}
Let $M = \lam{a}{a\,b}$ for distinct atoms $a$ and $b$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\ren{M}{a}{b} = \lam{b}{b\,b}$
    \lean{\DemoSrc\#L84}: renaming identifies the two atoms, so that
    the free $b$ is captured.
  \item $\sw{a}{b} \act M = \lam{b}{b\,a}$
    \lean{\DemoSrc\#L91}: the swap exchanges them.
  \item $\subst{M}{b}{a} = \lam{c}{c\,a}$ for a fresh atom $c$
    \lean{\DemoSrc\#L97}: substitution replaces only the free
    occurrence of $b$, and renames the binder to avoid capturing $a$.
\end{itemize}
\end{example}

Definition~\ref{def:subst} is, case for case,
Gabbay's~\cite[Definition~5.13]{gabbay2011a}: a function chooses the
new atom fresh for $a$, $b$ and the atoms of $M$ and $N$, and the proof
that the definition is well founded is his induction on the size of
the term, which renaming preserves~\cite[Lemma~5.14]{gabbay2011a}.
Gabbay renames the binder with a substitution $M[b \mapsto c]$, which
coincides with $\ren{M}{b}{c}$ since $c$ is fresh. Like Curry's
substitution~\cite[Definition~C.1]{barendregt1985a}, it is defined on
all terms, but it renames $b$ whenever $b$ is free in $N$, whereas
Curry keeps $b$ also when $a$ is not free in $M$. Renaming and substitution, a notion of alpha
equivalence (Definition~\ref{def:simp} below) and its basic theory,
including the substitution
lemma~\cite[2.1.16]{barendregt1985a}
\lean{\CroleSrc/Named/Untyped/Properties.lean\#L971},
are due to Yin and
Montesi\footnote{\url{https://github.com/leanprover/cslib/pull/458}}
and are the starting point of our work: the alpha equivalence of CSLib
is the reference point against which the other definitions are shown
equivalent in Section~\ref{sec:alpha-equivalences}.
