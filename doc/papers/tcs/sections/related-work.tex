\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Mechanized treatments of $\alpha$-equivalence as a subject in its own
right are few. Crole mentions an Isabelle/HOL~\cite{nipkow2002a} development of the
results of~\cite{crole2012a} as work in
progress~\cite[Section~7]{crole2012a}; we could find no published
account of it.
Apinis and Ahman~\cite{apinis2025a} define
$\alpha$-equivalence of untyped $\lambda$ terms as an inductive relation in the
Rocq Prover and show that it conforms to the specification given in
the literature. Their subject is one definition and its adequacy,
whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence} compares definitions
that the literature itself uses.

The isomorphism of Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn} was first mechanized
by Norrish and Vestergaard in HOL4~\cite{norrish2007a}, and
Figure~\ref{fig:equivalence-net} shows the further relationships
between formalizations of the $\lambda$-calculus that they list.

More broadly, several general-purpose approaches to variable binding
exist. Nominal techniques~\cite{pitts2013a}, as implemented in Nominal
Isabelle~\cite{urban2005a}, provide binder-generic reasoning
principles via permutations and freshness. Locally nameless
representations~\cite{aydemir2008a} aim to make binder-heavy
formalizations more tractable.
CSLib instead represents named and de Bruijn terms directly as
inductive types and proves the relationships between them explicitly,
as in Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn}.
