\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Dedicated mechanizations of $\alpha$-equivalence are rare. In his
concluding section, Crole reports that he and a student are formalizing his
proofs in Isabelle/HOL~\cite{nipkow2002a} for a follow-up
paper~\cite[Section~7]{crole2012a}. We could not find any published
account of it.

Apinis and Ahman~\cite{apinis2025a} define
$\alpha$-equivalence of untyped $\lambda$-terms directly by inference
rules in the Rocq Prover. They show that it is an equivalence and a
congruence, preserves free variables and is compatible with
substitution~\cite[Sections~4 and~6]{apinis2025a}. Their work concerns
one new definition and its use, whereas Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence}
compares several definitions that the literature itself uses.
Berghofer and Urban~\cite{berghofer2007a} compare formalizations in
Isabelle/HOL based on de Bruijn indices and on nominal names on two
case studies, among them the substitution
lemma~\cite[Sections~2 and~3]{berghofer2007a}. Crole describes this as
comparing definitions of $\alpha$-equivalence through their
applications~\cite[Section~7]{crole2012a}, whereas
Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence} compares them directly.

Norrish and Vestergaard mechanized the isomorphism of
Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn} in HOL4~\cite{norrish2007a}.
Figure~\ref{fig:equivalence-net} shows the further relationships
between formalizations of the $\lambda$-calculus that they list.
Earlier, Gordon~\cite{gordon1994a} built name-carrying terms in HOL on
top of de Bruijn terms, so that equality is $\alpha$-conversion by
construction. No relation on raw named terms is involved. Apinis and
Ahman~\cite[Section~11]{apinis2025a} also define $\alpha$-equivalence
of named terms through a translation to de Bruijn terms and compare the
proof obligations with those of their direct relation.

Many other approaches to variable binding have been proposed. The
POPLmark challenge~\cite{aydemir2005a} posed a set of benchmark
problems to compare them in mechanized metatheory.
Nominal techniques~\cite{pitts2013a},
implemented in Nominal Isabelle~\cite{urban2005a}, reason about binders
through permutations and freshness. Locally nameless
representations~\cite{aydemir2008a} use names for free variables and
de Bruijn indices for bound ones. CSLib contains a locally nameless
representation as well~\cite[Section~5.2]{henson2026a}. This paper
connects two other representations, the named and the de Bruijn one,
and proves the relationships between them explicitly
(Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn}).
