\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lambda terms are identified up to the names of their
bound variables. For terms with named variables this identification is
a relation on terms, i.e. $\alpha$-equivalence. 
Several definitions of $\alpha$-equivalence are found in the literature.
The textbook definition renames
bound variables: a subterm $\lam{a}{M}$ may be replaced by
$\lam{a'}{\subst{M}{a}{a'}}$, where $a'$ must not occur in $M$ at
all~\cite[Definition~2.1.11]{barendregt1985a} or must not occur free
in it~\cite[Definition~1.17]{hindley2008a}. Two terms are
$\alpha$-equivalent if a finite sequence of such replacements turns one into
the other. Gabbay and Pitts~\cite[Proposition~2.2]{gabbay2002a}
instead compare two abstractions $\lam{a}{M}$ and $\lam{b}{N}$
directly: they swap both $a$ and $b$ with the same fresh variable $z$
and compare the resulting bodies $\sw{z}{a} \act M$ and
$\sw{z}{b} \act N$. It is widely accepted that these
definitions agree.  Crole~\cite{crole2012a} proves in detail that
several such relations coincide.

The details are easy to get wrong. In the 1988 edition of their
textbook, Hindley and Seldin warned that ``the above technicalities
are rather dull. But their very dullness has made them a trap for even
the most careful authors'' (quoted in~\cite[p.~2]{crole2012a}). The
current edition only sketches the proofs about $\alpha$-equivalence, ``as
they are straightforward and boring''~\cite[p.~277]{hindley2008a}. 
Exercise~A1.9 of \cite{hindley2008a} shows that, contrary to a claim
in~\cite[p.~91]{curry1958a}, a single $\alpha$-contraction cannot always be reversed by one step.
Crole finds the short proof sketch of Gabbay and
Pitts~\cite[Proposition~2.2]{gabbay2002a} ``not quite right'', since
it lacks a connection between swapping and
substitution~\cite[p.~5]{crole2012a}. A formalization that checks every
step and stays close to the sources removes such doubts.

Another way to deal with the names of bound variables is to do without
them. In de Bruijn's representation~\cite{debruijn1972a} a bound variable is a numeral that
counts the binders between its occurrence and the binder it refers to.
That nameless terms are isomorphic to named terms modulo
$\alpha$-equivalence is stated, for instance,
in~\cite[Appendix~C]{barendregt1985a}. Norrish and
Vestergaard~\cite{norrish2007a} give a proof mechanized in HOL4~\cite{slind2008a}, for
pure de Bruijn terms with the $\beta$-reduction of
Nipkow~\cite{nipkow2001a}.

This paper presents formalizations of both results in the Lean~4
proof assistant~\cite{demoura2021a}. They were developed for CSLib~\cite{demoura2026a}, a
community-maintained library of computer science for Lean~4 built on
Mathlib~\cite{mathlib2020a}. CSLib collects reviewed and maintained formalizations of
standard results on which later research can build directly. Both
results extend CSLib's named representation of the untyped
$\lambda$-calculus, which Section~\ref{sec:cslib} recalls.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Section~\ref{sec:alpha-equivalences} formalizes Crole's
    theorem. Relations on named terms defined by swapping and by a
    renaming axiom are equal
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence}), whereas two further
    variants of the side condition define different relations
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:variants}). To our knowledge the theorem
    had not been mechanized before. The formalization follows Crole's
    proof and supplies the arguments that~\cite{crole2012a} omits or
    only sketches (Table~\ref{tab:gaps}).
  \item Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn} formalizes the isomorphism of
    Norrish and Vestergaard between named terms modulo
    $\alpha$-equivalence and de Bruijn terms, with their respective notions of
    $\beta$-reduction (Theorem~\ref{thm:db-isomorphism}). The quotient
    is taken by the $\alpha$-equivalence of CSLib, which is the first of
    Crole's relations. As an application, the Church--Rosser
    theorem for de Bruijn terms yields the Church--Rosser theorem for
    named terms modulo $\alpha$-equivalence
    (Corollary~\ref{cor:church-rosser}). This result is not new as a
    mechanization. We include it to connect the two
    representations of $\lambda$ terms within one library.
\end{enumerate}

The statements of Sections~\ref{sec:cslib}
to~\ref{sec:de-bruijn} carry a \raisebox{0.9ex}{\leanicon} symbol that links to the
corresponding declaration in the Lean sources. A
blueprint\footnote{\url{https://chris-anto-froeschl.github.io/untyped-lambda-calculus/}}
presents both formalizations together, with a dependency
graph\footnote{\url{https://chris-anto-froeschl.github.io/untyped-lambda-calculus/blueprint/dep_graph_document.html}}
that shows how they are connected.

For Section~\ref{sec:alpha-equivalences}, Aristotle~\cite{achim2025a},
an AI system for automated theorem proving in Lean, was used during
the exploration of alternative definitions and auxiliary swap lemmas.
The final proofs were substantially rewritten, manually reviewed, and
checked against the original paper. A similar verification workflow was used for the
formalization in Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn}, where
AI assistance was also used to compare the formalization with the
existing HOL4 development of Norrish and Vestergaard~\cite{norrish2007a}.

Both results are meant as parts of a library on which further work on
the untyped $\lambda$-calculus can build.
Section~\ref{sec:related-work} discusses related work,
Section~\ref{sec:future-work} describes the directions we have in
mind, in particular the remaining formalizations of the network of
Norrish and Vestergaard~\cite[Figure~1]{norrish2007a}, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
