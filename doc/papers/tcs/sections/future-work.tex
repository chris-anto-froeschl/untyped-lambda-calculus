\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}

We see three directions in which this work can be continued.

\emph{The network of Figure~\ref{fig:equivalence-net}.} $\LamA$ is the
quotient of the raw terms by $\simp$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence}, $\LamA$ is equally the
quotient by $\simr$ and by $\simrf$, the relations of
Barendregt~\cite[Definition~2.1.11]{barendregt1985a} and of
Hindley and Seldin~\cite[Definition~1.17]{hindley2008a}. Each
remaining $\alpha$-quotient edge of the figure thus reduces to
identifying the $\alpha$-equivalence of a raw calculus with one of
these relations. For $\lambda^{C}$, Vestergaard takes the equivalence
generated by Curry's rule with a fresh
atom~\cite[Section~1.2]{vestergaard2003a}. It has the form of
$\simrf$ but uses Curry's substitution instead of that of
Definition~\ref{def:subst}, so the two substitutions have to be shown
to agree up to $\alpha$-equivalence. For $\lambda^{V}$, Vestergaard and
Brotherston use a substitution that never renames, together with a
side condition that excludes capture~\cite[Figures~1 and~2]{vestergaard2003b}.
This relation appears to contain $\simr$ and to be contained in
$\simrf$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence} it would then equal
all of them, but we have not checked this formally.

The remaining isomorphisms need further representations: Gordon's
two-sorted de Bruijn terms $\mathrm{dB}_2$~\cite{gordon1994a} and
Vestergaard's raw calculus $\lambda^{V}$, whose isomorphism with
$\lambda^{C}$ Vestergaard has mechanized in Coq~\cite[Section~2.3]{norrish2007a}.
Formalizing these nodes and edges would bring the whole network of
Norrish and Vestergaard into one library.

\emph{Further equivalence results.} Results in the style of Crole,
which show that several natural but superficially different definitions
of the same notion coincide, are not unique to $\alpha$-equivalence. We
plan analogous formalizations for the SK combinator calculus. We also
plan to connect further binding representations, such as CSLib's
locally nameless terms and nominal terms, to the named representation
by explicit isomorphisms in the style of Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn}.

\emph{Teaching.} Fabrizio Montesi (University of Southern Denmark) and Alexandre
Rademaker (Getulio Vargas Foundation, Rio de Janeiro), both CSLib
maintainers, have expressed interest in using the named
representation of Section~\ref{sec:cslib} to teach the
$\lambda$-calculus and to base assignments on it. It suits this purpose
because it stays close to textbook presentations. It has terms with
named variables and capture-avoiding substitution, and through
Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence} the definitions of
$\alpha$-equivalence found in the
literature~\cite{barendregt1985a,hindley2008a} are available side by
side and proved equivalent. To support such use, we plan documentation
and examples aimed at students.
