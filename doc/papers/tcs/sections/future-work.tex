\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}

We see three directions in which this work can be continued.

\emph{The network of Figure~\ref{fig:equivalence-net}.} The alpha equivalence by which $\LamA$ is formed is $\simp$,
the same relation by which the HOL4 development underlying
\cite{norrish2007a} quotients its raw terms.\footnote{Rule
\texttt{aeq\_lam} in \texttt{examples/lambda/basics/generic\_termsScript.sml}
of the HOL4 sources.}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence}, $\LamA$ is equally the
quotient by $\simr$ and by $\simrf$, the relations of
Barendregt~\cite[Definition~2.1.11]{barendregt1985a} and of
Hindley and Seldin~\cite[Definition~1.17]{hindley2008a}. Since
substitution on $\LamA$ is lifted from raw terms
(Definition~\ref{def:lam-alpha}), the construction is also that of
$\lambda^{Ho}_{\alpha}$, which Norrish and Vestergaard describe as a
quotient of Curry's raw calculus $\lambda^{C}$ with substitution lifted
to the quotient~\cite[Section~2]{norrish2007a}; the raw terms of
Section~\ref{sec:cslib}, with the substitution of
Definition~\ref{def:subst}, are such a calculus. Homeier's alpha
equivalence, however, is a contextual relation defined without
swapping or substitution~\cite[Definitions~9 and~10]{homeier2001a},
so this edge, too, requires identifying it with one of the five.
The alpha equivalence of $\lambda^{C}$, as Vestergaard presents it,
is the congruence generated by Curry's rule with a fresh
atom~\cite[Section~1.2]{vestergaard2003a}. It has the form of
$\simrf$ but is stated with Curry's substitution instead of that of
Definition~\ref{def:subst}, so identifying the two requires showing
that both substitutions agree up to alpha equivalence. The alpha
equivalence of $\lambda^{V}$, mechanized by Vestergaard and
Brotherston~\cite{vestergaard2003b}, uses a substitution that never
renames, together with a side condition that excludes capture. It
appears to contain $\simr$ and to be contained in $\simrf$, which by
Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence} would make it equal to all five;
we have not yet checked this formally.

Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence} thus reduces each remaining
$\alpha$-quotient edge of the figure to identifying the alpha
equivalence of a raw calculus with one of the five relations, and
the remaining isomorphisms to further representations: Gordon's
two-sorted de Bruijn terms $\mathrm{dB}_2$ and Vestergaard's raw
calculus $\lambda^{V}$, whose isomorphism with $\lambda^{C}$ is
mechanized in Coq~\cite[Section~2.3]{norrish2007a}. Formalizing these
nodes and edges would bring the whole network of Norrish and
Vestergaard into one library.

\emph{Further equivalence results.} Crole-style results, showing several natural but superficially
different definitions of the same notion to coincide, are not unique
to alpha equivalence. We plan analogous formalizations for the SK
combinator calculus and other binding representations (locally
nameless terms, nominal terms), each intended to slot into CSLib
alongside the results of this paper and to be connected to them by
explicit isomorphisms in the style of Section~\ref{sec:de-bruijn}.

\emph{Teaching.} Fabrizio Montesi (University of Southern Denmark) and Alexandre
Rademaker (Getulio Vargas Foundation, Rio de Janeiro), both CSLib
maintainers, have expressed interest in using the named
representation of Section~\ref{sec:cslib} to teach the
lambda calculus and to base assignments on it. It suits this purpose
because it stays close to textbook presentations: terms with
named variables, capture-avoiding substitution, and, through
Theorem~\ref{thm:crole-equivalence}, definitions of alpha
equivalence found in the
literature~\cite{barendregt1985a,hindley2008a}, all available side by
side and proved equivalent. Supporting such use, with documentation
and examples aimed at students, is part of our plans for the
library.
